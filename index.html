Dragon Tiger Card Rules and Round Structure

How Dragon and Tiger sides are compared, and what determines each round's result

The Basic Concept

Dragon Tiger is one of the simplest card games available on digital platforms. Each round involves a single card dealt to two positions — Dragon and Tiger. The higher card determines which position wins the round. There are no player hands to build, no strategy beyond choosing which position to back, and no multi-step card evaluation. A single comparison between two cards decides every round.

Card Ranking

Cards are ranked from lowest to highest. Suit does not affect the ranking — a King of Hearts and a King of Spades are equal in value. Only the rank matters for comparison. Aces are typically ranked as the lowest card in Dragon Tiger, unlike in many other card games where Ace is highest. This is worth confirming in the specific game's rules as some platforms treat Aces differently.

Ties

When Dragon and Tiger cards have the same rank

If both positions receive a card of identical rank — for example, both receive a 7 regardless of suit — the round is a tie. Tie outcomes are typically handled with a specific rule set that differs from Dragon or Tiger outcomes. A common arrangement is that a tie result in a partial return on Dragon and Tiger positions. Tie as a specific position to back is available in many Dragon Tiger games and carries a different payout than Dragon or Tiger. Always check the specific game's tie rules before relying on assumptions.

Round Structure

  1. Betting phase: Players back a position — Dragon, Tiger, or Tie — before cards are dealt. A countdown timer shows how long the betting window remains open.

  2. Dealing: One card is placed face-down on the Dragon position; one on the Tiger position. Both are revealed simultaneously at the end of the dealing phase.

  3. Comparison: The two cards are compared by rank. The position with the higher card wins.

  4. Result declaration: Dragon wins, Tiger wins, or Tie is declared. Results and outcomes are applied to positions backed before the deal.

  5. Next round: A brief interval between rounds allows time for new selections before the next betting window opens.

Common Terminology

Position

Dragon or Tiger — the two sides of the comparison. "Backing a position" means choosing which side you expect to win before the deal.

Tie

When both cards share the same rank. Typically treated as a separate outcome from Dragon win or Tiger win.

Suit

The card's symbol category (hearts, spades, diamonds, clubs). Does not affect rank comparison in standard Dragon Tiger.

Rank

The card's numerical or face value — the single factor that determines which position wins each round.

Betting window

The time period before each deal during which positions can be backed. Once closed, no changes can be made for that round.

Paytable

The game's information screen showing what each position outcome produces. Check this for tie rules and any variations specific to the game you are playing.
